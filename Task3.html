4.1 Objective

In Task 2, two CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) guides, 200forw and 248forw, were selected to target the first exon of OAS2 on chromosome 12 using CRISPOR predictions (Haeussler et al. 2016). Because human genomes differ from the reference assembly, genetic variation can alter CRISPR guide performance in two ways:

  1. On-target guide availability: Variants within the target gene may alter the 20-base protospacer or disrupt the 3-base PAM, preventing guide recognition in certain individuals.
  2. Off-target binding risk: Variants within predicted off-target loci across the genome may increase or decrease sequence similarity or alter canonical PAM motifs.

Task 3 evaluates both questions across seven individuals from the 1000 Genomes Project (HG00142, HG00178, HG00237, HG01521, HG03129, NA19175, and NA19462) to refine the guide selection from Task 2.

4.2 Approach

Extracting Individual Genotypes from VCF Datasets

Variant Call Format (VCF) files record reference (REF) and alternate (ALT) alleles, genomic coordinates, and phased individual genotypes (0 for the reference allele, 1 for the first alternate allele, separated by | for phased haplotypes) (Danecek et al. 2011). Two complementary VCF sources were used to cover both the intended on-target locus on chromosome 12 and predicted off-target loci on chromosomes 1 and 2:

  • On-target check (Chromosome 12): Because OAS2 resides on chromosome 12, the GRCh38 phased VCF from the 1000 Genomes Project (ALL.chr12.shapeit2_integrated_snvindels_v2a_27022019.GRCh38.phased.vcf.gz) was subsetted to the OAS2 exon 1 region and the seven target individuals (chr12_OAS2_7samples.vcf). To match the pre-processed chromosome 1 and 2 files, sites where all seven individuals are homozygous for the reference allele (0|0, \(\text{AC} = 0\) in the subset) were filtered out so that only polymorphic variants carried by the seven individuals were retained. The two 23-base target-plus-PAM regions on the forward strand (chr12:112,978,698–112,978,720 for 200forw and chr12:112,978,746–112,978,768 for 248forw) were screened against the Task 2 Ensembl GRCh38 reference sequences.
  • Off-target check (Chromosomes 1 and 2): The CRISPOR off-target prediction table from Task 2 was filtered for 200forw and 248forw and compared against the provided 7-individual VCF subsets (sampled_chr1.vcf and sampled_chr2.vcf).

Sequence Partitioning and Haplotype Evaluation

In accordance with the SpCas9 recognition mechanism, each 23-base interval was partitioned into its 20-base protospacer and 3-base PAM (canonical NGG) (Haeussler et al. 2016). Mismatches were counted strictly across the 20-base protospacer, while PAM mutations were assessed separately. Using phased genotypes (0|0, 0|1, 1|0, 1|1), both maternal and paternal haplotypes were reconstructed for each individual to distinguish heterozygotes (one altered copy) from alternate homozygotes (two altered copies).

4.3 Findings

On-Target Guide Availability in Individual Genomes (Chromosome 12)

Screening the chromosome 12 OAS2 target intervals across the seven individuals (chr12_OAS2_7samples.vcf) revealed zero polymorphic variants carried by any of the seven individuals in either 200forw or 248forw (Table 4.1). Although four rare population variant records overlap the 248forw protospacer in the full 2,504-person 1000 Genomes release, all seven study individuals carry the homozygous reference genotype (0|0) at every position.

Effect on Off-Target Binding Risk

Partitioning the 23-base off-target locus at chr1:224,887,199 shows that the G > A substitution occurs at the third base of the PAM, mutating the canonical AGG PAM to a non-canonical AGA PAM (Table 4.4). The 20-base protospacer is unchanged and remains at 4 mismatches relative to 248forw.

Because SpCas9 strongly prefers a canonical NGG PAM for initial DNA recognition, replacing AGG with AGA reduces the likelihood of off-target dCas9 binding on haplotypes carrying the alternate allele, though low-level recognition at non-canonical NGA PAMs cannot be completely ruled out (Haeussler et al. 2016).

Evaluating phased genotypes reveals distinct individual-level profiles (Table 4.5):

  • Reference homozygotes (0|0, 3 individuals): HG00178, HG00237, and HG01521 retain the canonical AGG PAM on both chromosome 1 copies.
  • Heterozygotes (0|1 or 1|0, 3 individuals): HG00142, HG03129, and NA19175 carry the disrupted AGA PAM on one haplotype, but still retain the canonical AGG PAM on their second haplotype.
  • Alternate homozygote (1|1, 1 individual): NA19462 carries the AGA PAM on both chromosome 1 copies, eliminating the canonical NGG PAM at this locus entirely.

4.4 Discussion

Combining the chromosome 12 on-target analysis with the chromosome 1 and 2 off-target analysis directly refines the Task 2 guide selection across the seven personal genomes:

  1. Both candidate guides remain viable for on-target OAS2 regulation: Neither 200forw nor 248forw is affected by genetic variation in OAS2 exon 1 among the seven individuals. Both guides retain 100% protospacer identity and canonical TGG PAMs on both chromosome 12 haplotypes.
  2. Genotype-specific off-target refinement: Guide 200forw exhibits uniform behaviour across all seven genomes, with no variants intersecting its evaluated off-target intervals. For guide 248forw, the chr1:224,887,199 variant (G > A) does not create a new off-target risk; instead, it disrupts the canonical AGG PAM (AGG \(\rightarrow\) AGA) without reducing the four existing protospacer mismatches. However, because three of the four carriers (HG00142, HG03129, NA19175) are heterozygous, they still retain one functional AGG off-target copy, whereas only NA19462 (1|1) loses the canonical PAM on both chromosomes.
  3. Implications for CRISPRi: Because this therapeutic strategy employs CRISPRi (dCas9 transcriptional repression) rather than nuclease cleavage (Jensen et al. 2021), off-target risk primarily concerns unintended transcriptional silencing if dCas9 binds near a promoter or enhancer, rather than double-stranded DNA breaks. Preserving zero mismatches at the intended OAS2 first exon while maintaining high mismatch counts (4 mismatches) and disrupted PAMs at off-target loci supports the safety and efficacy of both guides.

Two methodological limitations should be noted. First, VCF files report variant calls relative to GRCh38 rather than base-by-base sequencing depth, meaning uncalled regions are assumed to match the reference genome. Second, screening was performed on reference-predicted off-target intervals; personal variants elsewhere in the genome can occasionally generate de novo NGG PAMs that would only be captured by a genome-wide variant-aware search (Lessard et al. 2017).

4.5 Conclusion

Across the seven evaluated 1000 Genomes individuals, chromosome 12 variant screening confirms that both 200forw and 248forw target sites in OAS2 exon 1 are 100% conserved (0|0) on both haplotypes. Off-target screening across chromosomes 1 and 2 identified a single SNP (chr1:224,887,199 G>A) affecting a predicted 248forw off-target site in four individuals, mutating its PAM from AGG to AGA while preserving four protospacer mismatches. Both 200forw and 248forw remain strong candidates for OAS2 CRISPRi modulation, with 200forw showing complete invariance across all assessed loci and 248forw experiencing a haplotype-dependent reduction in canonical PAM availability at one off-target site.

read_zipped_vcf <- function(zip_path, chrom_label) {

  ) |>

    ) |>
    select(chrom, POS, REF, ALT, all_of(samples))
}

  }
  str_sub(seq, rel_idx, rel_idx) <- str_sub(alt, 1L, 1L)
  seq
}

## Discussion

Combining the chromosome 12 on-target analysis with the chromosome 1 and 2 off-target analysis directly refines the Task 2 guide selection across the seven personal genomes:

1.  **Both candidate guides remain viable for on-target *OAS2* regulation:** Neither **200forw** nor **248forw** is affected by genetic variation in *OAS2* exon 1 among the seven individuals. Both guides retain 100% protospacer identity and canonical `TGG` PAMs on both chromosome 12 haplotypes.
2.  **Genotype-specific off-target refinement:** Guide **200forw** exhibits uniform behaviour across all seven genomes, with no variants intersecting its evaluated off-target intervals. For guide **248forw**, the `chr1:224,887,199` variant (`G > A`) does not create a new off-target risk; instead, it disrupts the canonical `AGG` PAM (`AGG` $\rightarrow$ `AGA`) without reducing the four existing protospacer mismatches. However, because three of the four carriers (`HG00142`, `HG03129`, `NA19175`) are heterozygous, they still retain one functional `AGG` off-target copy, whereas only `NA19462` (`1|1`) loses the canonical PAM on both chromosomes.
3.  **Implications for CRISPRi:** Because this therapeutic strategy employs CRISPRi (dCas9 transcriptional repression) rather than nuclease cleavage [@jensen2021], off-target risk primarily concerns unintended transcriptional silencing if dCas9 binds near a promoter or enhancer, rather than double-stranded DNA breaks. Preserving zero mismatches at the intended *OAS2* first exon while maintaining high mismatch counts (4 mismatches) and disrupted PAMs at off-target loci supports the safety and efficacy of both guides.

Two methodological limitations should be noted. First, VCF files report variant calls relative to GRCh38 rather than base-by-base sequencing depth, meaning uncalled regions are assumed to match the reference genome. Second, screening was performed on reference-predicted off-target intervals; personal variants elsewhere in the genome can occasionally generate *de novo* `NGG` PAMs that would only be captured by a genome-wide variant-aware search [@lessard2017].

## Conclusion

Across the seven evaluated 1000 Genomes individuals, chromosome 12 variant screening confirms that both **200forw** and **248forw** target sites in *OAS2* exon 1 are 100% conserved (`0|0`) on both haplotypes. Off-target screening across chromosomes 1 and 2 identified a single SNP (`chr1:224,887,199 G>A`) affecting a predicted **248forw** off-target site in four individuals, mutating its PAM from `AGG` to `AGA` while preserving four protospacer mismatches. Both **200forw** and **248forw** remain strong candidates for *OAS2* CRISPRi modulation, with **200forw** showing complete invariance across all assessed loci and **248forw** experiencing a haplotype-dependent reduction in canonical PAM availability at one off-target site.
