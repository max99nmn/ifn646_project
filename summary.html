5  Summary

This project integrates transcriptomic differential expression analysis (Task 1), computational CRISPR guide RNA design (Task 2), and personal genome variant screening (Task 3) into a unified, reproducible R workflow.

Task 1: Consensus Differential Expression Analysis

To identify genes deregulated during SARS-CoV-2 infection (SARS vs. mock at 5 days post-infection) while controlling for baseline inter-donor variance (~ donor + condition), raw RNA-seq counts were analysed using two complementary Negative Binomial Generalized Linear Model frameworks: DESeq2 (median-of-ratios normalisation, empirical Bayes dispersion shrinkage, and Wald testing) and edgeR (filterByExpr pre-filtering, TMM normalisation, and Likelihood Ratio Testing).

At a Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted threshold of \(0.05\), DESeq2 identified 68 significant genes and edgeR identified 7 significant genes. Applying a conservative intersection with strict fold-change sign concordance yielded six high-confidence consensus genes: five upregulated (OAS2, GPRASP1, AC026801.2, IFI27, and CXCL11) and one downregulated (SERPINB2). Prioritising method intersection over a union or p-value meta-analysis minimised tool-specific false positives prior to downstream guide design.

Task 2: Target Selection and CRISPRi Guide Design

From the six consensus candidates, OAS2 (ENSG00000111335) was selected over AC026801.2 (non-coding lncRNA), GPRASP1 (zero baseline mock counts and no primary antiviral role), SERPINB2 (downregulated, modest effect size), and IFI27/CXCL11 due to its strong, concordant induction (\(\log_2\text{FC} = 9.90\) in DESeq2, \(10.30\) in edgeR) and its direct enzymatic role in the 2’-5’-oligoadenylate synthetase/RNase L innate antiviral and hyperinflammatory pathway (Merold et al. 2025; Gao et al. 2023; Hernandez-Ramirez et al. 2026). Because OAS2 is upregulated in SARS-CoV-2 infection, CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) using dCas9-KRAB transcriptional repression was selected to restore expression toward baseline without inducing permanent double-stranded DNA breaks (Jensen et al. 2021).

Using the canonical MANE Select transcript (ENST00000392583.7) on the forward strand of chromosome 12 (GRCh38.p14) (Morales et al. 2022), the 267 bp first exon (ENSE00002421397.2, chr12:112,978,519–112,978,785) was screened in CRISPOR (20bp-NGG, SpCas9) (Concordet and Haeussler 2018; Haeussler et al. 2016). Two forward-strand guides were selected after excluding any candidates with 0-, 1-, or 2-mismatch off-targets:

  • 200forw (chr12:112,978,698–112,978,720): Selected for the highest MIT specificity (97), high CFD specificity (96), strong Doench 2016 (64) and Moreno-Mateos (48) efficiency scores, and 52 predicted off-targets (0-0-0-4-48).
  • 248forw (chr12:112,978,746–112,978,768): Selected as a complementary candidate matching the top CFD specificity (96) and total off-target count (52, 0-0-0-5-47), while achieving the highest RuleSet3 efficiency score (89) and solid MIT specificity (89).

Task 3: Personal Genome Refinement Across Seven Individuals

To evaluate how human genetic variation impacts on-target guide availability and off-target binding risk (Lessard et al. 2017), both guides were screened in R across seven individuals from the 1000 Genomes Project (HG00142, HG00178, HG00237, HG01521, HG03129, NA19175, and NA19462) (Danecek et al. 2011):

  1. On-target conservation in OAS2 (Chromosome 12): Subsetting the IGSR GRCh38 chromosome 12 phased VCF (chr12_OAS2_7samples.vcf) to the seven study individuals and filtering out population-only records (0|0 across all seven samples) demonstrated zero polymorphic variants in either 200forw or 248forw. All seven individuals are homozygous reference (0|0) across both target intervals, retaining zero protospacer mismatches and an intact TGG PAM on both chromosome 12 haplotypes.
  2. Off-target binding risk (Chromosomes 1 and 2): Intersecting the exported CRISPOR off-target intervals (offtargetsguide.txt) with the 7-individual chromosome 1 and 2 VCF subsets (sampled_chr1.vcf.zip and sampled_chr2.vcf.zip) identified zero overlapping variants for 200forw and one overlapping SNP for 248forw at chr1:224,887,199 (G > A).
  3. Haplotype-specific PAM disruption: Separating the 23-base off-target locus into its 20-base protospacer and 3-base PAM showed that the chr1:224,887,199 G>A substitution leaves the protospacer unchanged at four mismatches but mutates the canonical AGG PAM to a non-canonical AGA PAM, reducing predicted dCas9 binding affinity on alternate haplotypes (Haeussler et al. 2016). Among the seven genomes, three individuals (HG00178, HG00237, HG01521) are homozygous reference (0|0, retaining AGG / AGG), three (HG00142, HG03129, NA19175) are heterozygous (0|1 or 1|0, carrying AGG / AGA), and one (NA19462) is homozygous alternate (1|1, carrying AGA / AGA).

5.1 Implications and Methodological Limitations

Stage Key Takeaway Primary Limitation & Future Direction
Task 1 (Transcriptomics) Consensus DE modelling (DESeq2 \(\cap\) edgeR) isolates robust interferon-stimulated gene induction (OAS2, IFI27, CXCL11). Only two biological donors (\(n = 2\)) and near-zero mock counts inflate unshrunken \(\log_2\text{FC}\) magnitudes; validation in larger patient cohorts is required.
Task 2 (gRNA Design) Pairing 200forw (top MIT/Doench) and 248forw (top RuleSet3/CFD) in OAS2 exon 1 balances specificity and efficiency. Efficiency models are trained primarily on Cas9 cleavage rather than dCas9-KRAB repression; empirical knockdown assays are needed to confirm steric TSS repression.
Task 3 (Personal Genomes) Both guides retain 100% on-target conservation on chr12; 200forw is invariant across screened off-targets, while 248forw loses a canonical off-target PAM on ALT alleles. VCF files do not verify base-level sequencing depth across reference calls, and reference-based off-target lists do not capture de novo variant-created NGG PAMs elsewhere in the genome.

5.2 Overall Conclusion

Across all three analytical stages, 200forw and 248forw emerge as well-supported candidate gRNAs for CRISPRi-mediated modulation of OAS2. Both guides are 100% conserved at their intended chromosome 12 target sites across all seven evaluated human genomes, with 200forw offering the highest genome-wide specificity and complete invariance across screened off-target regions, and 248forw providing a high-efficiency alternative whose single overlapping off-target SNP (chr1:224,887,199 G>A) disrupts a canonical AGG PAM in four carriers. Together, these findings demonstrate how integrating multi-tool transcriptomics with haplotype-aware genomic screening refines precision CRISPR therapeutics prior to experimental validation.
